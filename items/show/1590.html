Convention of Colored People of Ohio. We have received an account of the proceedings of this Convention, which sat in this city the whole of last week; but the crowded state of our colums, at this period, compels us to substitute for the report as communicated, a brief editorial abstract. The Convention was organized, Monday morning, August 21st, 1837, by appointing the Rev. C. Yancy President, Wm. Johnson and J. Malvin, Vice Presidents, A.M. M. Summer, J. Leach, and L.L. L. Lewis, Secretaries, and W. Shelton, Chaplain. Committees were appointee to draft reports on different subjects connected with the objects of the Convention; but it was subsequently determined that every thing should originate and be considered in the Committee of the whole. One of the earliest resolutions was worthy of a much much more enlightened body of men; it limited the speeches on subjects that might incidentally come up to fifteen minutes each. We have not a copy of the Report of the Select Committee on the objects of the Convention. We understand its principal features to have been a recommendation for the establishment, by and among the colored people of Ohio, of a School Fund and Mutual Improvement Institution. Accordingly, we find the following resolution among the proceedings of the present day: -- "Resolved, That as a School Fund and Mutual Improvement Institution, for the moral and religious elevation of the colored people of Ohio, be formed."
